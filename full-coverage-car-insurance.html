FULL COVERAGE GUIDE

What “full coverage” car insurance usually means.

“Full coverage” is a common phrase, but it is not one universal insurance product. People often use it to describe a policy that combines liability insurance with collision and comprehensive coverage. The exact protections, limits, exclusions and deductibles still depend on the policy.

Liability coverage

Liability coverage can help pay for injuries or property damage you are legally responsible for after a covered crash, subject to the policy terms and limits. State minimum requirements vary and may not match the amount of protection an individual driver wants.

Collision coverage

Collision coverage generally helps pay for covered damage to your insured vehicle after a collision, subject to a deductible. It is typically optional under state law, although a lender or lessor may require it while a vehicle is financed or leased.

Comprehensive coverage

Comprehensive coverage generally addresses certain non-collision losses such as theft, fire, weather or other covered events, subject to the policy terms and deductible. It is also commonly required by lenders on financed or leased vehicles.

Deductibles change both price and risk

A higher deductible can reduce a premium, but it also increases the amount you may need to pay after a covered loss. Compare insurers using the same deductible so the price comparison remains meaningful.

“Full coverage” does not mean unlimited coverage

Every policy still has limits, exclusions and conditions. Optional protections such as rental reimbursement, roadside assistance, gap coverage or uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage may or may not be included. Review the actual declarations and policy documents before relying on a label.
