DEDUCTIBLE GUIDE

Compare deductibles before comparing premiums.

A lower monthly premium can look attractive, but a higher deductible means more out-of-pocket exposure after certain covered claims. When comparing comprehensive and collision coverage, try to compare the same deductible amount across insurers.

What a deductible does

A deductible is generally the amount a policyholder pays toward a covered comprehensive or collision loss before the insurer pays its portion, subject to the policy terms. Liability coverage usually works differently, so the deductible concept should not be applied to every part of a policy.

Lower deductible

A lower deductible can reduce the amount you would need to pay after a covered claim, but it may come with a higher premium. Whether the trade-off is worthwhile depends on the price difference, your financial situation and the coverage terms.

Higher deductible

A higher deductible can sometimes lower the premium, but it increases the amount you may need to pay yourself after a covered loss. Before choosing a higher deductible, consider whether that amount would be affordable on short notice.

Why matched deductibles matter

Comparing a policy with a $250 deductible against another with a $1,000 deductible can make the second option look cheaper even though the out-of-pocket exposure is very different. For a fairer comparison, align both the coverage limits and deductibles whenever possible.

Do not choose on premium alone

Review the deductible together with liability limits, comprehensive and collision coverage, optional protections, exclusions, fees and the insurer's final verified terms. A quote should be judged as a package, not just by one monthly number.
